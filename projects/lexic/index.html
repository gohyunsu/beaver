01 · Foundations

Reading gaze reflects both the reader and the text.

A long fixation may indicate difficulty, importance, novelty, or a reader’s strategy. A gaze-only model observes the behavioral result but may not know which words were intrinsically difficult.

Text propertieslength · frequency · surprisal

Change how predictable and costly a word is to process.

→
Reading behaviorfixation · skip · regression

Combines lexical difficulty with reader ability and strategy.

→
Outcomequestion response

Provides an imperfect measure of whether the passage was understood.

Word length
A surface measure correlated with visual and morphological processing cost.
Word frequency
How often a word appears in a reference corpus; rare words are often processed more slowly.
Surprisal
−log P(word | previous context). Higher surprisal means the word was less expected in context.
Project hypothesis

Precomputed lexical features can supply text context at deployment without running a large language model alongside every prediction.

Approach

Two ways to add lexical context.

LEXIC-Concat

Direct feature fusion

Concatenate word length, frequency, and GPT-2 surprisal to the gaze feature associated with each fixated word.

Strength · simple and reproducible
LEXIC-Res

Typical-reader residual

Predict expected gaze from lexical difficulty, then condition the comprehension model on the difference between expected and observed behavior.

Risk · the “typical” reader may not transfer
Unseen reader

New person, familiar text distribution

Unseen text

Familiar readers, new passage

Unseen both

New reader and new passage

02 · Evidence

A small signal exists; its meaning remains open.

The current preprint reports K=5 seed ensembles over ten folds. Both lexical variants improve unseen-text AUROC by approximately 1.8–2.2 percentage points, and Concat improves unseen-reader AUROC by about 2.9 points.

Observed
  • Small AUROC gains
  • Concat is the more stable design
  • Residual transfer is weaker
Possible interpretation
  • Lexical context explains some gaze-only error
  • Simple fusion may be sufficient
  • Typical-reader gaze may be reader-specific
Not established
  • Practical decision value
  • Feature-level attribution
  • Other datasets or backbones
  • Deployment-cost advantage
Precise wording

GPT-2 surprisal is computed in advance. The method is not “language-model-free”; the defensible claim is “no language-model inference at deployment” or “precomputed lexical conditioning.”

Because lexical feature fusion is well established, the strongest contribution is not architectural novelty. It is a controlled empirical account of which inexpensive cues help under which generalization shift, at what computational and calibration cost.

Direction and progress

Turn a small gain into an explanatory study.

The initial model and evaluation exist. The revision question is whether the gain can be attributed, generalized, calibrated, and justified by cost.

Attribute

Feature matrix

Length, frequency, surprisal, all three pairwise combinations, and all features under identical folds and seeds.

Generalize

Model and dataset

Repeat on at least one different gaze backbone and, where compatible, another EyeBench task or corpus.

Calibrate

Decision quality

Add AUPRC, balanced accuracy, ECE, Brier score, and subgroup error analysis.

Measure

True cost

Separate one-time feature precomputation from deployment parameters, latency, memory, and operations.

Working research question Which precomputed lexical cues improve gaze-based comprehension prediction under reader and text shift, and are the gains calibrated and inexpensive enough to matter?
