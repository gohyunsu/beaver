GazeMed/Foundations

Chapter 01 · Foundations

From eye movement to evidence.

GazeMed connects three domains that use similar words in different ways: medical imaging, eye tracking, and machine learning. This chapter establishes the common vocabulary.

In this chapter 1. The localization task 2. Chest X-ray coordinates 3. Gaze representations 4. Speech and temporal alignment 5. What a fixation can mean 6. Weak supervision

01

First, distinguish classification, localization, and segmentation.

A model can answer several different questions about the same chest X-ray. The output contract determines what counts as a correct prediction.

ClassificationIs pleural effusion present?

One score for the image. No location is required.

LocalizationWhere is the mentioned effusion?

A point, box, ellipse, or probability map identifies a region.

SegmentationWhich exact pixels belong to it?

A pixel-level boundary is expected.

GazeMed is a localization study.

REFLACX ellipses are coarse reference regions drawn by radiologists. They are not pixel-perfect lesion masks, so “segmentation” would overstate the target.

The unit of prediction

One GazeMed instance is not simply an image. It is a reading × finding pair: a particular radiologist interpreting a particular image, with one finding mention linked to one or more reference ellipses.

one reading+one finding mention+its reference region=one localization instance
Checkpoint

If two radiologists read the same image, they produce two readings—and potentially different gaze paths, reports, and ellipses.

02

Spatial words only make sense inside a coordinate system.

A frontal chest X-ray is displayed as if the viewer were facing the patient. The patient’s right side appears on the viewer’s left. Pixel coordinates, however, normally begin at the top-left of the displayed image.

Three frames can coexist

Image frame
Pixel x/y measured from the top-left.
Patient frame
Left/right and superior/inferior described clinically.
Anatomical frame
Relations such as retrocardiac, hilar, basilar, or adjacent to the pleura.

Simple directional tokens are useful, but radiology language is often relational. “Retrocardiac opacity” is not merely left or lower; it identifies a region behind the heart. “Bibasilar” combines two sides with a vertical zone. A robust model must eventually represent these compositions.

Common failure

A left/right feature can be internally consistent and still be clinically reversed if the image and patient frames are mixed.

03

Raw gaze, fixations, scanpaths, and heatmaps are not interchangeable.

An eye tracker produces a rapid stream of samples. Most modeling begins by compressing that stream into events or spatial summaries.

Raw sample(x, y, t, pupil)

A single tracker measurement. High temporal resolution also preserves noise, blinks, and redundant points.

Fixationstable gaze over an interval

A cluster summarized by mean position, start/end time, and duration.

Saccademovement between fixations

A rapid transition. Its path and velocity may reflect search behavior but are harder to model reliably.

Scanpathordered fixation sequence

Retains where the reader looked and in what order.

Heatmapspatial density

Aggregates gaze over space. Easy to use, but usually discards order and phase.

Why bandwidth matters

A point becomes a heatmap by spreading mass around its coordinate, often with a Gaussian kernel. A wider kernel creates a smoother and larger region, which can improve overlap with a coarse ellipse even when the underlying gaze has not improved.

H(p) = Σᵢ wᵢ · exp(−‖p − gᵢ‖² / 2σ²)

gᵢ is a fixation coordinate, wᵢ is its weight, and σ controls spatial smoothing. Representation comparisons must use matched or explicitly tuned bandwidths.

GazeMed choice

The current model uses fixation events. An early raw-trajectory comparison did not retain an advantage after spatial smoothing was matched.

04

Seeing and saying are synchronized—but not simultaneous.

A radiologist generally observes evidence before dictating it. The delay varies with the finding, reader, sentence structure, and whether the gaze reflects discovery or verification.

Fixed lag versus learned alignment

A fixed-lag heuristic selects gaze from a hand-chosen interval, such as 1.5 seconds before a mention. A learned model instead assigns a relevance weight to each fixation from its temporal relationship to the mention.

Fixed window

One delay for every case

Simple and reproducible, but brittle when speaking patterns differ.

Learned alignment

A distribution over fixations

Can represent variable delays, but must be tested against timestamp shuffling and future-gaze leakage.

Causal boundary

Using gaze at inference time is not sufficient to call a model real-time. A real-time claim also requires future fixations to be structurally unavailable.

05

A fixation records orientation, not intention.

The same location can be viewed for different reasons. Treating every fixation near a lesion as diagnostic evidence creates label noise at the level of meaning, even when the coordinates are accurate.

Detect

Evidence first becomes salient.

Potentially useful for discovery-oriented localization.

Verify

A suspected finding is checked again.

Often occurs near dictation and may be easier to align.

Routine

Anatomy is inspected systematically.

Can reproduce common scan patterns without indicating abnormality.

Distract

A visually salient region is rejected.

Attention is present, but positive supervision would be wrong.

REFLACX provides coordinates and timestamps, not gaze-role labels. GazeMed therefore learns a latent relevance weight. Revisit patterns, speech timing, negative statements, and small expert annotations could help determine whether these roles are identifiable.

Interpretation rule

“Looked at” should never be translated directly into “diagnosed” without additional evidence.

06

Gaze is weak supervision because it is informative and imperfect.

Strong supervision directly labels the target—for example, a carefully traced lesion mask. Weak supervision provides a related signal that is cheaper or naturally produced, but noisy.

Weakgaze densitycheap, indirect, temporally rich
rough box or ellipselocalized, coarse boundary
Strongpixel maskprecise, expensive, not always well-defined

What GazeMed learns

The target ellipse evaluates where the abnormality was marked. Gaze and speech provide the inference input. The model’s task is not to reproduce every fixation, but to select the subset that best predicts the finding’s reference region.
