Beyond directional words: how should spatial language shape GazeMed?

Inference-time gaze and speech define a credible research direction. Spatial language can make that direction stronger—but only if it becomes a structured, anatomy-aware constraint and is evaluated against simpler explanations.

01 · Position

The short answer

The central idea is worth pursuing. Most closely related work uses gaze to construct supervision or improve an image model during training. GazeMed instead keeps a new radiologist’s gaze and speech in the inference contract. That is a meaningful distinction.

The spatial-language pathway is also promising, but the current result should be treated as a positive probe, not a finished language contribution. Seven binary location features improve regional overlap reliably; their effect on the binary Pointing metric is smaller and less stable. The next step should therefore deepen the representation and its controls, not enlarge the overall architecture indiscriminately.

A five-step mental model

Before considering architecture details, it helps to see GazeMed as a sequence of five decisions. Each decision turns a continuous clinical interaction into a testable localization problem.

01Observe

Record where the radiologist’s gaze stabilizes while a chest X-ray is being interpreted.

02Listen

Timestamp the spoken report and identify the phrase that mentions one finding.

03Align

Estimate which fixations are relevant to that mention from timing, position, and language.

04Localize

Place the learned attention weights back at the radiologist’s real fixation coordinates.

05Test

Compare the resulting heatmap with the radiologist-drawn ellipse for that finding.

Fixation

A short interval in which gaze is sufficiently stable to be treated as one observed location. It is not the same as every raw eye-tracker sample.

Mention

The timestamped phrase associated with one finding label—for example, a phrase describing pleural effusion.

Ellipse

A coarse region drawn after interpretation to indicate the finding’s location. It is evaluation ground truth, not an input at inference.

Instance

One reading–finding pair with a present label, at least one ellipse, the full fixation sequence, and a matched report mention.

Recommended direction Preserve the simple gaze–speech attention mechanism. Replace flat location keywords with anatomy-grounded spatial frames, evaluate them causally, and first prove value beyond a finding-specific anatomy prior.

02 · Current evidence

How the two metrics read the same heatmap differently

Region overlap

Intersection over Union · IoU

area(prediction ∩ ellipse) / area(prediction ∪ ellipse)

The heatmap is thresholded into a region. IoU rewards a region that overlaps the ellipse without spreading too far outside it. The threshold and smoothing bandwidth are selected on validation data, then frozen for test.

Peak hit

Pointing game

1 if argmax(heatmap) lies inside ellipse; otherwise 0

Pointing asks only whether the single hottest location hits the target. It is threshold-free, but binary: a useful reshaping of the rest of the heatmap is invisible if the peak remains on the same side of the ellipse boundary.

Consequently, IoU 0.3188 does not mean that 31.88% of cases were “correct.” It is the mean degree of regional overlap. Pointing 0.7237 is a proportion: the peak landed inside the target on 72.37% of the seed-0 test instances.

The RadZero row is a reference point, not a modality contest. RadZero sees image pixels and a phrase; GazeMed sees gaze and speech but not image pixels. On the frozen split, Final has higher IoU (p = 1.6×10−4) and is not detectably different on Pointing (p = 0.92), where RadZero’s point estimate is slightly higher. The defensible conclusion is competitive localization from a different input contract, not superiority over image-based grounding.

Result version note

An earlier project summary quoted 0.233 for the fixed REFLACX baseline. The latest PR technical specification reports 0.2082 after the conditions were aligned on the current 1,093-instance protocol. Results should be cited from one frozen specification rather than mixing stages of the experiment.

The controls ask why Final improves

The important result is not only that Final scores higher. Two information-removal controls damage a specific correspondence while leaving most of the trained system unchanged:

  • Position shuffle: permuting x/y among fixations within each instance reduces IoU to 0.2101 and Pointing to 0.4337. The coordinate set is still present, but each location is attached to the wrong temporal and linguistic context. The collapse shows that the true correspondence is essential.
  • Spatial-word mask: removing the seven location descriptors reduces IoU to 0.2943 and Pointing to 0.7118. Across five training seeds and five patient splits, the IoU effect is positive and significant in 10/10 runs. Pointing moves in the same direction in 10/10 but reaches significance in 5/10.

This supports a strong position-correspondence claim and a smaller, metric-dependent spatial-language claim. Masking may reshape the region enough to change IoU without moving the single hottest point across the ellipse boundary. Treating the two controls as equally strong would obscure the most interesting next question.

One run is not the headline

The often-quoted Pointing value 0.7237 is the maximum of the five training seeds, not the typical result. A project summary should use the mean with its range; the seed-0 table should remain available for paired statistical comparisons.

03 · Claim boundary

Test-time gaze is novel here. “Real-time” still needs a causal test.

The project’s clean distinction is that it uses gaze and speech for a new case at inference. That should remain central. It is more precise than saying previous work “does not use gaze at inference,” because the literature contains several interactive and broader gaze-conditioned systems; the direct comparison should be limited to the closest CXR localization setting.

What a synchronized reading looks like

REFLACX records several clocks at once. Eye-tracker samples are parsed into stable fixations; dictated words receive timestamps; zoom, pan, and window state are recorded; and the finding ellipse is drawn only after dictation for validation. GazeMed joins these streams at the level of one finding mention.

Available to the model

Full fixation sequence, word timing, matched finding text, and finding identity.

Used only for evaluation

The hand-drawn finding ellipse. The model must not see this target when producing a test prediction.

Not used by current GazeMed

Chest X-ray pixels. This is why RadZero is a competitiveness reference, not an input-matched baseline.

However, the current implementation receives the full fixation sequence and includes a signed time difference between each fixation and the mention. A fixation after the spoken phrase can therefore participate in attention. This does not invalidate the localization result, but it changes the operational claim.

A causal replay should define an output clock—such as the end of a finding phrase—and enforce fixation_end ≤ output_time. It should report:

  1. localization performance at the output clock,
  2. the fraction of mentions with enough prior gaze to produce a map,
  3. latency from phrase completion to prediction, and
  4. the gap between causal-prefix and full-sequence results.

The critical comparison is not merely “with future gaze” versus “without future gaze.” It should also show when a correct region first becomes stable as the phrase unfolds. That connects localization quality to operational latency.

This experiment may reveal that post-mention gaze is valuable verification rather than leakage. If so, the project should distinguish an online discovery mode from an offline or delayed verification mode instead of forcing both into one real-time claim.

04 · Current representation

What the spatial-language pathway does now

The implementation does not embed an unrestricted report sentence. It detects a finding mention with keyword rules and turns the matched sentence into ten binary descriptors.

Spatial · 7left · right · bilateral · upper · lower · middle · retrocardiac
Extent · 3small-type · large-type · diffuse-type

From a report sentence to an attention score

1 · Mention matchingWhich sentence refers to this finding?

Keyword regular expressions associate a finding label with timestamped report sentence(s). A negation guard removes straightforward absence statements.

2 · QueryWhat should the model look for?

The 10 descriptor bits are concatenated with an 8-dimensional learned finding embedding and projected into one query vector.

3 · Fixation keysWhat does each candidate gaze point carry?

Each fixation contributes six temporal/kinematic features, raw x/y, and the same finding embedding.

4 · Cross-attentionWhich fixation best matches the request?

A single scaled dot-product attention layer produces a probability distribution over the fixation sequence.

The six temporal/kinematic features are signed time to the nearest mention, absolute time difference, a before-mention flag, a legacy 1.5-second-window flag, fixation duration, and instantaneous velocity. The model can therefore learn a soft alternative to the fixed gate without discarding the physical meaning of its inputs.

Implementation detail

The circulated example included “top” and “below.” The current code operationalizes the related concepts as upper and lower; it does not contain a separate below descriptor. This matters because “lower” is a vertical zone, while “below” is a relation between two entities.

Mention matching is part of the measurement

If a regex attaches the wrong sentence—or misses a paraphrase—both the timing features and the descriptor vector become noisy at once. A manual mention-link audit is therefore not merely data cleaning; it is a validity check for the language claim.

This design is excellent as a mechanism probe: it is inspectable, easy to mask, and difficult to confuse with a large pretrained language model. It is less suitable as the final representation of radiological space.

05 · Opportunity

Why spatial language can add something that gaze alone cannot

1. Gaze produces candidates; language can select among them.

A complete reading contains many fixations unrelated to the current finding. If a radiologist has inspected both lung bases, the word “right” can select a subset. If several findings share a region, the finding identity and timing can further disambiguate them.

2. Language can express a coordinate system.

Raw x/y tells the model where a point lies in the image. Language tells it how the clinician organizes that location: left versus right, apical versus basilar, hilar versus peripheral, or behind the heart. These concepts can remain stable across image dimensions and modest viewing transformations.

3. Relations are more informative than isolated directions.

Radiology frequently describes a finding relative to anatomy. Rad-SpatialNet models spatial language with frames containing a trigger and related elements. This offers a natural bridge from a sentence to a constraint over the chest.

The diagram makes the limitation of a flat keyword vector concrete. “Extends into” can describe where a device terminates or how a disorder spreads; “projects over” can indicate depth or termination level depending on the entities involved. The model must represent who is located relative to what, not only whether a trigger word occurred.

4. Language may identify when gaze should be ignored.

Negated, uncertain, comparative, and follow-up statements do not have the same localization meaning as a new positive finding. A structured language pathway can learn or enforce abstention rather than merely shifting a heatmap.

The main confound

A finding label already predicts a typical anatomical distribution. Spatial words may simply sharpen that prior. GazeMed must show that patient-specific gaze adds value beyond a label-conditioned anatomy map before interpreting language–gaze attention as individualized evidence.

06 · Proposed extension

Move from a keyword vector to an anatomy-grounded spatial frame

A useful intermediate representation should be richer than binary keywords but more auditable than a black-box sentence embedding.

A practical representation pipeline

01 · Detect
Find the finding mention and its time span.

Retain the word-level timestamps instead of reducing the entire sentence to one time point.

02 · Parse
Build a typed spatial frame.

Extract the finding, anatomical anchor, relation, extent, polarity, and certainty. Preserve unresolved slots rather than guessing.

03 · Ground
Convert the frame into an anatomy-aware prior.

Map “behind the heart” to a retrocardiac region using an anatomical atlas or separately validated anatomy model—not the test ellipse.

04 · Refine
Use temporally eligible gaze to personalize the prior.

Score only the fixations available by the output clock and let their real coordinates sharpen or contradict the language prior.

05 · Decide
Produce a region, confidence, or abstention.

Negated, uncertain, unmatched, or poorly supported frames should be allowed to return “insufficient evidence.”

The model can convert the anchor and relation into an interpretable spatial prior, then ask whether temporally relevant gaze refines it. This yields a clear decomposition:

finding prior→anatomy + relation prior→patient-specific gaze refinement

Each arrow becomes a measurable increment. If language alone already localizes the case, gaze should not receive credit. If gaze only helps after the relation is known, the interaction itself becomes the contribution.

Keep the anatomy prior honest

An anatomy mask used at inference must come from a fixed atlas, a separately trained anatomy model, or image annotations available under the declared input contract. Building it from test finding ellipses would leak the answer into the baseline.

Why not begin with a general sentence encoder?

A pretrained encoder may improve scores while hiding whether it learned anatomy, severity, finding identity, or report style. A structured frame supplies coverage statistics, explicit failure modes, controllable ablations, and a clean target for later representation learning.

07 · Evaluation plan

Six experiments that would make the direction convincing

01 · Coverage

Descriptor and relation audit

Report how often each current descriptor fires, its manual precision, co-occurrence, laterality errors, and finding distribution.

Answers: Is the measured language effect broad or driven by a few common phrases?
02 · Baseline

Finding-conditioned anatomy prior

Create maps from training ellipses only. Compare label-only, label+direction, label+anatomical frame, Temporal, and Final on matched cases.

Answers: Does patient-specific gaze beat typical location?
03 · Attribution

Descriptor-level leave-one-group-out

Remove laterality, vertical zone, anatomy anchor, relation, extent, and uncertainty groups separately.

Answers: Which language family actually changes localization?
04 · Correspondence

Cross-instance language swap

Exchange spatial frames between compatible findings while preserving label and model capacity.

Answers: Is the model using case-specific language or a generic finding cue?
05 · Causality

Incremental phrase replay

Update the query as words arrive and reveal only past fixations. Measure the first time a stable, correct region emerges.

Answers: When does language become useful in a live interpretation?
06 · Generalization

Compositional holdout

Hold out selected finding–relation or anchor–relation combinations while retaining their components in training.

Answers: Does the representation compose, or memorize phrase templates?

These tests are more informative than comparing several large encoders on one split. They reveal whether language supplies a reusable spatial abstraction and whether gaze provides patient-specific residual information.

Run them as evidence gates, not a feature backlog

Gate A · MeasurementCan the current language signal be trusted?

Complete the mention-link and descriptor audit first. If coverage or precision is poor, improve the measurement before comparing new encoders.

Gate B · Added informationDoes gaze beat an anatomy prior?

If label+frame performs as well as label+frame+gaze, the contribution is language-derived localization rather than patient-specific gaze grounding.

Gate C · Causal availabilityDoes the effect survive prefix replay?

If performance depends heavily on post-mention fixations, position the system as delayed verification and stop making a live-output claim.

Gate D · RepresentationDo structured frames generalize?

If held-out relation combinations fail, retain an auditable rule/frame system and gather targeted annotations before adding a general sentence encoder.

A result should be allowed to stop an extension. That is the practical value of these gates: they determine whether the next unit of effort belongs in data validation, causal evaluation, spatial semantics, or multimodal fusion.

08 · Recommendation

A coherent direction for the project

Established stronglyPosition + language improves over timing alone.

The ordering Final > Temporal-only > B1 holds on both metrics across five training seeds and five patient splits. Position shuffle fails decisively.

Established with qualificationSpatial words add a small regional signal.

The masking effect is consistent and significant for IoU, but the binary Pointing metric detects it only about half the time across the combined runs.

Still openReal-time causality and value beyond anatomy.

Full-sequence evaluation does not prove causal online availability, and a label/frame-conditioned anatomy prior has not yet been isolated.

Keep

The small finding-conditioned cross-attention, real fixation splatting, and information-removal controls.

Clarify

Describe the current system as test-time gaze + speech localization until causal prefix replay is complete.

Strengthen first

Add a label-conditioned anatomy prior, a manual mention-link audit, and causal prefix replay. Training-seed and patient-split sensitivity are already measured.

Extend next

Replace binary spatial descriptors with anatomy-grounded frames and evaluate them incrementally.

Expand only if supported

Explore image–behavior fusion after per-instance errors show meaningful complementarity.

The most defensible contribution is therefore not “a model that adds gaze to a large medical vision system.” It is a controlled account of how a radiologist’s gaze, timing, and spatial language jointly ground a spoken finding, with explicit tests for the simpler explanations that could produce the same result.

RadZero adds one useful boundary: GazeMed is stronger on IoU and not detectably different on Pointing under the frozen split, despite using a different input modality. That makes future image–behavior fusion plausible, but it does not show that gaze is better than image evidence. Fusion becomes justified only when case-level errors are complementary rather than merely different on average.

Working research question When a radiologist describes a finding, how much patient-specific localization information is provided by the timing and position of their gaze beyond the finding’s anatomical prior, and can structured spatial language select that information causally?
