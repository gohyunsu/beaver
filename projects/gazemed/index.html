The problem

A report says what. Gaze may help recover where.

A chest X-ray report usually describes findings in language, while many downstream tasks need an image region. During interpretation, gaze and speech are naturally synchronized but do not align perfectly.

01Inspect

A radiologist moves through anatomical regions and candidate abnormalities.

02Decide

03Dictate

The model must identify which earlier fixations correspond to that mention.

Core distinction

GazeMed does not ask whether gaze can train a later image-only model. It asks whether a new radiologist’s gaze and speech can serve as inputs for localization during that interpretation.

Current approach

Learn which fixation belongs to a finding mention.

The current model forms a query from a finding and compact language descriptors, compares it with every fixation, then places the resulting attention weights back at the recorded gaze coordinates.

Learned timing beats a fixed lag. True fixation positions matter. Masking spatial words reduces both metrics across all nine distinct seed and split runs.
