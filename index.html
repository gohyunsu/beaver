BEAVER Research Program

We study the traces left by human judgment.

Where people look, what they say, and how they act reveal different parts of a decision. BEAVER asks when those traces provide useful information to an intelligent system—and when they are only noise, habit, or distraction.

Research program

One question, examined across three settings.

A signal is useful only when it explains something that a simpler source—an image, the text itself, or visual saliency—cannot already explain.

Central question What does a human signal add beyond the task context that produced it?
Observe

Capture a trace

Measure gaze, language, or action without assuming what it means.

Interpret

Name the context

Separate attention from intent, difficulty, verification, and habit.

Compare

Test added value

Use strong priors and information-removal controls before adding complexity.

Translate

Define a useful role

Turn a supported signal into supervision, assistance, or explanation.

Principles

How a claim becomes credible.

01

Do not equate observation with intent.

A fixation records visual orientation. Its role must be established with timing, task context, and later behavior.

02

Compare against the strongest simple explanation.

Center bias, anatomy, saliency, text difficulty, and image-only models are part of the question—not optional baselines.

03

Remove information, not only modules.

Shuffle time, position, order, or language while preserving capacity to test what the model actually uses.

04

Keep the boundary of evidence visible.

Observed results, supported interpretations, open alternatives, and future ideas should never collapse into one claim.
