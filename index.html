Understanding the traces left by human judgment.

A person does not leave only a final answer. Their eyes move, words unfold, and actions arrive in sequence. BEAVER studies what those traces reveal, what they fail to reveal, and when an intelligent system can use them responsibly.

01 · Research program

Begin with one distinction: a trace is not the mental state itself.

Attention, understanding, dissatisfaction, confidence, and intent are latent constructs: they cannot be observed directly. An eye tracker measures coordinates. A microphone records sound. An interface records clicks. These observations may contain evidence about a construct, but the meaning depends on the task and on alternative explanations.

A long fixation, for example, can mean difficulty, interest, comparison, verification, or distraction. The measurement is real; the interpretation remains a hypothesis. BEAVER turns that hypothesis into a sequence of testable steps.

ObserveCapture a trace

Record gaze, language, or action without assigning it a psychological meaning in advance.

OperationalizeDefine an observable target

Connect the construct to a later region, answer, choice, correction, or other checkable event.

CompareChallenge the simple explanation

Test saliency, anatomy, text difficulty, reader identity, and image-only priors first.

TranslateChoose a bounded role

Use supported signals for supervision, suggestions, explanations, or calibrated abstention.

Central question What does a human signal add beyond the image, text, task structure, and population regularities that produced it?

02 · Foundations

Human signals are useful because they are partial.

Each signal exposes a different slice of a decision. Gaze is spatially precise but semantically ambiguous. Language is semantically expressive but delayed and compressed. Action provides a clear behavioral commitment but often arrives after the system could have helped.

Attention trace

Gaze answers “where and when?”

Raw samples, fixations, dwell, revisits, and scanpaths describe visual orientation. They do not directly label diagnosis, comprehension, or intent.

Semantic trace

Language answers “what and in what relation?”

A report, phrase, or instruction names objects and relations, but may omit exact location and may follow observation after a variable lag.

Commitment trace

Action answers “what did the person choose?”

A click, answer, selected region, or accepted edit offers a stronger operational target, but it can reflect interface constraints as well as internal preference.

Task context

Context explains why the same trace changes meaning

A fixation during free viewing differs from one during diagnosis, information seeking, verification, or correction. Task state is part of the input contract.

The same signal can play different roles

Gaze can be an inference input, as in GazeMed; a candidate cue for an interface suggestion, as in GazeImageRefine; or a behavioral representation whose missing context is supplied by language features, as in leXic. Saying that all three projects “use gaze” hides the actual scientific distinction.

04 · What synchronized data looks like

Two datasets show why process data is richer than a final label.

REFLACX records a medical interpretation as image, gaze, dictation, and later localization. OneStop records reading under controlled goals and text difficulty, followed by comprehension questions. Both preserve the process that precedes an outcome.

Evidence principles

How a human-signal claim becomes credible.

The objective is not to demonstrate that gaze can be placed inside a model. It is to identify the conditions under which the signal changes a decision for the right reason.

01

Observation is not intent.

A fixation records orientation. Its role must be established with task context, timing, and later behavior.

02

Use the strongest simple baseline.

Center bias, anatomy, saliency, lexical difficulty, and image-only models belong in the main question.

03

Remove information, not only modules.

Shuffle time, position, order, or meaning while preserving capacity to test what a model actually uses.

04

Keep the evidence boundary visible.

Separate observations, supported interpretations, open alternatives, and future system ideas.
